## Legal and privacy conclusion

The reviewed Indian privacy provisions impose substantive duties; they do not
state that a lawyer must sign or approve a privacy notice, processor engagement,
or product release. The earlier “qualified legal review” gate is therefore an
ENTER release policy, not an identified statutory formality. In particular:

- the DPDP Act requires a lawful purpose, notice, clear affirmative consent where
  consent is used, proof of notice and consent, processor accountability under a
  valid contract, security safeguards, and appropriate erasure;
- overseas processing remains subject to any restrictions notified by the Indian
  Government; and
- the Act and 2025 Rules have phased commencement, so ENTER must recheck the
  operative requirements before later provisions take effect.

Google's Gemini API terms distinguish unpaid and paid services. For a paid API
project, Google states that prompts and responses are not used to improve its
products and are processed under the applicable Data Processing Addendum. Google
also states that paid-service prompts and responses may be logged for a limited
period for abuse prevention, security, and required legal disclosures, and may be
processed in countries where Google or its agents operate. ENTER must not describe
the ordinary paid service as zero-data-retention unless that separate status is
actually verified.

Primary references reviewed:

- [Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf)
- [MeitY DPDP Act and Rules publication page](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies)

## Minimum defensible ENTER approval process

Publication may proceed only after one accountable, dated release decision covers
the exact hashes above. ENTER may use either of these paths:

1. qualified legal clearance, accurately recording the reviewer's qualification
   and reference; or
2. management privacy/legal risk acceptance by the CEO, privacy owner, or another
   board-authorized officer, accurately recording authority and expressly stating
   that it is not independent qualified legal clearance.

The selected decision must confirm all of the following in one evidence record:

- the notice and optional unchecked consent are clear, specific, upload-scoped,
  withdrawable for future processing, and do not affect rule-based access;
- the paid Google project, applicable DPA, overseas processing, subprocessors,
  limited abuse-monitoring retention, and key-owner authorization were reviewed;
- Privacy v1.2 and Terms v1.2 accurately describe the processing and management
  accepts the residual liability and Bengaluru-jurisdiction wording;
- the real publication date, current token prices, daily/monthly application
  budgets, and the separate Google monthly cap are recorded; and
- only new, explicitly consenting uploads are eligible; existing or declined
  resumes are never sent to Gemini.

Independent counsel should be used where the accountable officer cannot resolve
a legal question, a restricted transfer is notified, the service expands to a
new jurisdiction or regulated use, or the provider contract no longer supports
the disclosures. That escalation rule is prudent internal governance, not a claim
that counsel approval is always legally mandated.
